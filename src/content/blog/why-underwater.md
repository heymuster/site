Fair question: how does anyone end up $10,000 underwater on a truck?

You buy a first-generation EV truck. Part of what you're paying for — part of what the
sticker price *means* — is the software future you're told is coming to your hardware:
hands-free driving, the autonomy roadmap, the truck that gets better while you sleep.

Then Gen 2 ships with a new autonomy platform, and your hardware generation quietly isn't
part of that future anymore. The features that justified the price never arrive on your
truck. They arrive on the next one.

The market does the rest. First-gen EV depreciation is already brutal; first-gen EV
depreciation *plus* a software roadmap that moved to newer hardware is how a truck can be
worth $10k less than what's owed on it. Nobody at the dealership mentions this curve when
you're signing.

So that's the hole. This isn't a sob story — I signed the papers, and early-adopter tax is
a real tax. But it's why the gap exists, why the truck's going to a better home, and why
the fleet has 30 days to earn the difference.
